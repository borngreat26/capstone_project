## Disease score methods

The 90 original TCSC/LDSC files contained Z-scores rather than valid PRS weights. None was scored.
The unused archives were removed after inventorying at the user's request on November 20, 2026;
the historical inventory and cleanup checksums are retained. Current analyses do not require them.
The primary analysis uses GWAS Catalog GRCh37 **formatted `.f.tsv.gz` files** for
Eyre et al. (2012; rheumatoid arthritis, GCST005569) and Mahajan et al. (2018;
type 2 diabetes, European BMI-unadjusted analysis, GCST007517), and the author-hosted
Pardiñas et al. (2018) CLOZUK+PGC2 schizophrenia meta-analysis. Its archive MD5 matches
the author checksum, A1/A2 and OR meanings are documented by the author, and its
IMPUTE2 identifiers are parsed to rsIDs. GRCh37 coordinates are verified using independently
identified panel rsID anchors (counts are in the SNP audit); mismatched positions are excluded.
The portal does not explicitly label this build, so this is an empirical build verification.
The corresponding EBI
`.h.tsv.gz` metadata describe GRCh38 harmonised files and must not be mistaken for the
build of the formatted files used here. RA weights are log(OR); T2D betas are documented
log-odds meta-analysis effects. Source URLs and file checksums are recorded in the repository.

The RA and T2D targeted Immunochip/exome-array studies have limited, nonrandom coverage. They are
educational disease-associated scores, not validated clinical predictors. The three selected diseases meet
the verified input requirements; the Z-only local files remain unsuitable. The schizophrenia
original was completely downloaded and checksum-verified before analysis. Original study
effects and extracted weights remain local because author redistribution conditions apply.

GWAS variants were matched by rsID with GRCh37 position checks; missing T2D rsIDs were
assigned only from unique panel positions. Nonambiguous reverse complements preserve
beta sign while relabeling the counted biological allele. Ambiguous, incompatible,
duplicated and invalid rows were excluded. Stage-specific counts are saved for each disease.
PLINK2 clumping used EUR reference LD, r²=0.1, 250 kb and a fixed p≤0.001 threshold.
Only threshold-eligible variants enter clumping, which is equivalent for retained index SNPs.
Scores sum log-odds beta × effect-allele dosage; missing calls cause an error, not silent
imputation. All people within each public score use the same variants. SD uses ddof=1.
Private comparisons are saved only under ignored `data/private_part2/`; both target and
reference are rescored on their common compatible, nonmissing SNP set. Their values and
plots do not enter this public report. A percentile is a reference rank, not a probability
of disease or an estimated relative risk.

On the same 489 people, RA and T2D standardized scores have Pearson
r=0.0802 (nominal p=0.07628) and Spearman r=0.0340
(p=0.4533). Their weak positive linear relationship does not establish a shared
causal mechanism. Four SNPs are shared (see `snp_overlap.tsv` for directional fractions
and Jaccard similarity). LD, selected variant overlap, discovery estimates and structure
can all influence these correlations.

## PCA methods and sensitivity analysis

PLINK2 used all 22 autosomes, MAF≥0.05, missingness≤0.02, HWE p≥10⁻⁶ and LD pruning
with a 200-variant window, 50-variant step and r²=0.2. Exact PCA of the variance-standardized
genetic relationship matrix used 96,333 variants and 489 public EUR individuals.
All 488 nontrivial eigenvalues were computed so explained fractions use the full eigenvalue
sum. PC1 explains 0.792% and PC2
0.353%. This describes within-EUR structure. Validated
population labels were not supplied, so clusters are unlabeled and no groups were inferred
from identifiers. The computation is unadjusted for pedigree relationships; LD pruning and
these basic filters do not exhaust PCA quality control.

Each standardized PRS was regressed on an intercept, PC1 and PC2 for the same people.
The RA–T2D residual Pearson correlation is 0.1060; this is an exploratory sensitivity
analysis, not proof that ancestry bias is removed. RA–PC1 correlation is recorded in
`prs_pc_associations.tsv`; nominal p-values are descriptive and are not corrected for
multiple exploratory comparisons. Private ancestry results were not calculated or published.

## Week 7 motivation and methods

[Roadmap Epigenomics (2015)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25693563/) maps cell-context
chromatin states; [ENCODE (2020)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-020-2493-4)
integrates biochemical measurements to identify candidate regulatory elements. Accessible
DNA can permit regulatory protein binding. ATAC-seq uses transposase integration to assay
accessibility, rather than measuring gene regulation or causality directly. B cells are
relevant to B-cell-derived lymphoblastoid cell lines (LCLs), but primary B cells differ from
EBV-transformed LCLs in activation, transformation and experimental context.

The existing ENCODE file ENCFF421XIL from ENCSR603LVR is primary B-cell ATAC-seq,
pseudoreplicated narrowPeak (BED6+4), originally GRCh38. Its uncompressed content MD5
matches official ENCODE metadata. UCSC liftOver using hg38ToHg19 and minMatch=0.95
retained 100,364/100,655 peaks and lost 291.
Original signal annotations were preserved; original summit offsets were not interpreted
as lifted summit coordinates. File metadata, chain checksum and mapping counts are saved
in `results/week7/provenance.json`. The file predated this work, so its original download
date is unknown; metadata were retrieved November 17, 2026.

BED intervals are [start,end), zero-based; one-based SNP p is represented by [p−1,p).
The saved 6,597,508 Part 1 SNP–gene associations were reused without refitting regressions.
BH and Bonferroni significance use the original genome-wide adjusted p<0.05. Unique
SNPs are counted separately from SNP–gene pairs, using ID/chromosome/position identity.
GENCODE v12 one-based inclusive gene bodies are converted to BED coordinates; gene-body
and ±500 kb window intersections are counted independently for all autosomal protein-coding
genes. Python interval counts support nested peaks, and boundary tests verify endpoints.

## Week 7 results

- **All tested:** 16,118/1,001,779 unique SNPs overlap (1.609%); separately, 185,689/6,597,508 SNP–gene associations overlap.
- **BH significant:** 3,027/80,939 unique SNPs overlap (3.740%); separately, 4,003/101,916 SNP–gene associations overlap.
- **Bonferroni significant:** 868/18,136 unique SNPs overlap (4.786%); separately, 983/20,172 SNP–gene associations overlap.

Among 19,189 annotated autosomal protein-coding genes, 14,733 have a gene-body peak and 19,008 have a peak in the ±500 kb window. This denominator includes genes without testable expression.

- **ERAP2:** lead rs2762 (p=1.64e-109) does not overlap a peak; 8 BH-significant and 8 Bonferroni-significant unique cis-SNPs overlap. Gene body: 9 peaks; cis-window: 58 peaks.
- **LDHC:** lead rs4757650 (p=1.11e-114) does not overlap a peak; 5 BH-significant and 5 Bonferroni-significant unique cis-SNPs overlap. Gene body: 1 peaks; cis-window: 51 peaks.
- **RPS26:** lead rs10876864 (p=4.94e-137) overlaps a peak; 9 BH-significant and 7 Bonferroni-significant unique cis-SNPs overlap. Gene body: 2 peaks; cis-window: 129 peaks.
- **MXRA7:** lead rs9303003 (p=1.07e-100) does not overlap a peak; 2 BH-significant and 2 Bonferroni-significant unique cis-SNPs overlap. Gene body: 4 peaks; cis-window: 130 peaks.

The case studies are the four strongest genes ranked by the saved Part 1 minimum p-value,
regardless of peak overlap. RPS26 overlaps accessible chromatin at its lead SNP, but this
does not identify a causal SNP or demonstrate that the peak regulates RPS26. A peak
anywhere within ±500 kb is a proximity annotation. Higher overlap among significant SNPs
is descriptive, without a formal enrichment claim: LD, allele frequency, local variant density
and distance from genes violate naive independence assumptions. No optional enrichment
test was performed. The regional figure is a Matplotlib plot, not LocusZoom software output.
